## Design Context

### Users
Volunteer coaches, parent coaches, and team managers running youth and grassroots football matches. Used **outdoors** — on a pitch, in daylight, often with one hand, under moderate stress. The primary device is a phone held at arm's length with the sun potentially creating glare. Users are not professionals: they're parents and club volunteers making substitution decisions in real time without dedicated coaching staff.

The job to be done: get to the right decision fast, with confidence, without having to think about the interface.

### Brand Personality
**Fast. Clear. Fair.**

- **Fast**: zero friction to reach any action mid-match
- **Clear**: no ambiguity about state, no hunting for controls
- **Fair**: the tool serves the players, not the coach's ego — rotation and fairness are built in

The emotional goal during a live match is **calm control under pressure**. The interface should *reduce* stress, not amplify it. When the coach looks at the screen, they should feel oriented and in control — not overwhelmed.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Light mode is primary.** Outdoor readability in bright conditions is a hard requirement. High contrast, no low-opacity treatments that wash out in sunlight.

Dark mode is fully supported and equally refined — but light is the default experience.

**Visual tone**: Functional confidence. Not corporate, not gamified. Closer to a well-designed physical tool (a good stopwatch, a coach's clipboard) than to a startup app or sports broadcast UI. Clean, purposeful, slightly serious.

**Anti-references** — FairPlayr must NOT feel like:
- Enterprise dashboards (Salesforce, Power BI) — no heavy admin chrome
- Gamified sports apps (FIFA-style) — no flashy effects, gradients, or broadcast aesthetics
- Legacy federation/club tools — no visual debt, no small crowded UI
- Overdesigned startup apps — no glassmorphism, blur, excessive animation, decorative layers

### Color System
**Lime green (`#9fe870`) is the one and only accent color.** It signals action and CTA. It earns its power by being rare — if everything is lime, nothing is.

**Neutrals carry the interface.** Grays, near-black, and warm whites handle all structure, hierarchy, and surface. No indigo. No secondary brand color. No color system beyond: lime + neutrals.

Derive hover, focus, and selected states from the lime palette or neutral tones only.

In dark mode, lime shifts to `#a3e635` for brightness compensation.

### Typography
**Geist Sans** is the confirmed font. It is purpose-built for interfaces — excellent legibility at small sizes, clean without being cold, functional without being corporate. Appropriate for a sideline tool.

No display font needed. This is a product UI, not a marketing surface. Use weight and size for hierarchy, not font switching.

### Design Principles
1. **Readability first** — outdoor, bright light, one hand, stressed coach. Every text size, contrast ratio, and touch target must be optimized for this context, not a desktop demo.

2. **Lime is signal, neutrals are structure** — the accent appears only where action is needed. It communicates "tap here." Everything else is neutral. When a coach sees lime, they know it matters.

3. **Calm through hierarchy** — information is ordered by what the coach needs *right now*. Critical state is immediately visible; secondary detail recedes. Nothing competes for equal attention.

4. **Predictable speed** — consistent placement, consistent behavior, consistent patterns. Muscle memory should work. The interface never surprises mid-match.

5. **Honest structure** — no decorative chrome. If an element can't justify its existence functionally, it doesn't exist. Clean, direct, trustworthy.
